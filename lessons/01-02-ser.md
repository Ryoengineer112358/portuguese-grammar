## The forms

*Ser* /ˈse(ʁ)/ means "to be" in the sense of **what or who someone is**. It is irregular, so the forms simply have to be learned — but there are only four you need in Brazil.

| Who | *ser* | IPA |
|---|---|---|
| eu | **sou** | /ˈso(w)/ |
| você · ele · ela · a gente | **é** | /ˈɛ/ |
| nós | **somos** | /ˈsõ.mus/ |
| vocês · eles · elas | **são** | /ˈsɐ̃w̃/ |

*É* is pronounced with an open /ɛ/, as in English *bed*. In Rio de Janeiro, *somos* ends in /ʃ/: /ˈsõ.muʃ/.

## Introducing yourself with *ser*

**1. Who you are.** *Eu sou a Ana* or simply *Sou a Ana* ("I'm Ana"). Brazilians often put *o* (for a man) or *a* (for a woman) in front of a first name in casual speech; *Eu sou Ana* is also understood.

**2. Where you're from: *ser de*.** "Where are you from?" is *De onde você é?* — literally "From where are you?" Answer with *Sou de* + place: *Sou de Tóquio*, *Sou de São Paulo*. Most country names take an article, which merges with *de*: *Sou **do** Japão*, *Sou **do** Brasil*. (These contractions are covered in lesson 03-05; for now, just learn your own country as a phrase.)

**3. Nationality and job — no "a/an".** English says "I'm *a* student," but Portuguese leaves out *um/uma* after *ser* with a profession or nationality: *Sou estudante*, *Ele é médico*, *Sou japonês*. Adding *um* sounds like you mean "a certain kind of…" and is used only when you describe the person further: *Ele é um médico muito bom* ("He's a very good doctor").

**4. The word after *ser* agrees with the person.** Nationality and many job words change for a woman and for a group: *Ele é brasileiro* / *Ela é brasileira* / *Eles são brasileiros*. You'll study this agreement in Chapter 6; for now, learn the forms that describe you.

## Watch out

> **No *um/uma* with jobs.** *Eu sou um engenheiro* is not how Brazilians state their job. Say *Eu sou engenheiro* or just *Sou engenheiro*.

> ***Ser* is not for how you feel.** "I'm tired" is *Estou cansado* /isˈto(w) kɐ̃ˈsa.du/, not *Sou cansado*. Temporary states use *estar*, the other "to be" — that's the next lesson.

> ***É* vs. *e*.** *É* /ˈɛ/ ("is") has an accent; *e* ("and") has none and is pronounced /i/. *Sou engenheiro. E você?* — "And you?"

<details markdown="1">
<summary>1. How do you say "I'm from Brazil"?</summary>

*Sou do Brasil.* (or *Eu sou do Brasil.*)
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>2. What's unnatural about <em>Eu sou um estudante</em>?</summary>

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>3. Fill in the blank: <em>Eles ___ brasileiros.</em></summary>

*são* — *Eles são brasileiros.*
