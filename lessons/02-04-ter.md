## The forms

*Ter* /ˈte(ʁ)/ ("to have") is irregular, so learn its four forms by heart:

| Who | ter |
|---|---|
| eu | **tenho**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈtẽ.ɲu/</span> |
| você · ele · ela · a gente | **tem**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈtẽj̃/</span> |
| nós | **temos**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈtẽ.mus/</span> |
| vocês · eles · elas | **têm**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈtẽj̃/</span> |

*Tem* and *têm* sound the same; the circumflex on the plural is only in writing.

## Four everyday jobs of *ter*

**1. Having things and people.**

- *Você tem irmãos?* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈtẽj̃ iʁˈmɐ̃w̃s/</span> — Do you have any brothers or sisters?

**2. Age.** Portuguese says you "have" years, not that you "are" them. *Anos* /ˈɐ̃.nus/ ("years") is part of the phrase:

- *Quantos anos você tem?* <span class="ipa">/ˈkwɐ̃.tus ˈɐ̃.nus voˈse ˈtẽj̃/</span> — How old are you?
- *Tenho trinta anos.* <span class="ipa">/ˈtẽ.ɲu ˈtɾĩ.tɐ ˈɐ̃.nus/</span> — I'm thirty.

**3. *Ter que* + infinitive = "have to."** This is the everyday way to say you must do something. (*Ter de* means the same but sounds more formal.)

- *Tenho que trabalhar amanhã.* <span class="ipa">/ˈtẽ.ɲu ki tɾa.baˈʎa(ʁ) a.mɐ̃ˈɲɐ̃/</span> — I have to work tomorrow.

**4. *Tem* = "there is / there are."** In Brazilian speech, *tem* with no subject means "there is" or "there are." It stays *tem* even before a plural:

- *Tem banheiro aqui?* <span class="ipa">/ˈtẽj̃ bɐ̃ˈɲe(j).ɾu aˈki/</span> — Is there a bathroom here?
- *Tem muita gente aqui.* <span class="ipa">/ˈtẽj̃ ˈmũj̃.tɐ ˈʒẽ.t͡ʃi aˈki/</span> — There are lots of people here.

In formal writing you'll see *há* /ˈa/ instead (*Há muita gente aqui*). It's correct, but in conversation it sounds bookish.

## Watch out

> **Age uses *ter*, not *ser*.**<br>
> ✗ *Eu sou trinta anos.*<br>
> ✓ *Eu tenho trinta anos.* <span class="ipa">/ˈew ˈtẽ.ɲu ˈtɾĩ.tɐ ˈɐ̃.nus/</span> — I'm thirty.

> **"There are" is still *tem*.** ✗ *Têm muita gente na festa.* ✓ *Tem muita gente na festa.* <span class="ipa">/ˈtẽj̃ ˈmũj̃.tɐ ˈʒẽ.t͡ʃi na ˈfɛs.tɐ/</span> — There are lots of people at the party. *Têm* is only for "they have."

> **Write *têm* for "they have."** *Eles têm dois filhos.* <span class="ipa">/ˈe.lis ˈtẽj̃ ˈdojs ˈfi.ʎus/</span> — "They have two kids." The circumflex is easy to forget because you can't hear it.

## Quick check

<details markdown="1">
<summary>1. How do you say "I'm twenty-five"? (<em>vinte e cinco</em> = 25)</summary>

*Tenho vinte e cinco anos.* <span class="ipa">/ˈtẽ.ɲu ˈvĩ.t͡ʃi i ˈsĩ.ku ˈɐ̃.nus/</span> — "I'm twenty-five."
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>2. Ask in a shop "Do you have water?" and "Is there water?" — what's the difference?</summary>

*Você tem água?* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈtẽj̃ ˈa.ɡwɐ/</span> — "Do you have water?" and *Tem água?* <span class="ipa">/ˈtẽj̃ ˈa.ɡwɐ/</span> — "Is there any water?" Without a subject, *tem* means "there is."
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>3. Say "I have to leave now." (<em>sair</em> = to leave, <em>agora</em> = now)</summary>

*Tenho que sair agora.* <span class="ipa">/ˈtẽ.ɲu ki saˈi(ʁ) aˈɡɔ.ɾɐ/</span> — "I have to leave now."
</details>
