## Asking: just raise your voice at the end

A yes/no question in Portuguese uses **exactly the same words** as the statement. There's no helper verb like English *do*, and the subject and verb don't swap places — only the pitch rises at the end (and you write a question mark).

| Statement | Question |
|---|---|
| *Você fala inglês.*<br><span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈfa.lɐ ĩˈɡle(j)s/</span><br>You speak English. | *Você fala inglês?*<br><span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈfa.lɐ ĩˈɡle(j)s/</span><br>Do you speak English? |
| *Ela tá em casa.*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈɛ.lɐ ˈta ẽj̃ ˈka.zɐ/</span><br>She's at home. | *Ela tá em casa?*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈɛ.lɐ ˈta ẽj̃ ˈka.zɐ/</span><br>Is she at home? |
| *Você é brasileiro.*<br><span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈɛ bɾa.ziˈle(j).ɾu/</span><br>You're Brazilian. | *Você é brasileiro?*<br><span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈɛ bɾa.ziˈle(j).ɾu/</span><br>Are you Brazilian? |

The IPA is identical in each row: the only difference is the rising intonation, which IPA transcriptions here don't mark.

## Three things to know

**1. The pronoun is often dropped.** When it's clear who you're asking, the question can start with the verb:

- *Fala português?* <span class="ipa">/ˈfa.lɐ poʁ.tuˈɡe(j)s/</span> — Do you speak Portuguese?

**2. Answer "yes" by repeating the verb — in your own form.** Brazilians rarely answer with a bare *sim* /ˈsĩ/ ("yes"). Instead, they repeat the verb from the question, changed to fit the person answering. A question with *você* gets an answer in the *eu* form:

- *— Você gosta de café? — Gosto.* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈɡɔs.tɐ d͡ʒi kaˈfɛ/ — /ˈɡos.tu/</span> — "Do you like coffee?" "I do."
- *— Você é casado? — Sou.* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈɛ kaˈza.du/ — /ˈso(w)/</span> — "Are you married?" "I am."
- *— Vocês são daqui? — Somos.* <span class="ipa">/voˈse(j)s ˈsɐ̃w̃ daˈki/ — /ˈsõ.mus/</span> — "Are you (all) from here?" "We are."

To say "no," put *não* before the same verb, usually with an extra *Não,* in front (lesson 01-05):

- *— Você gosta de café? — Não, não gosto.* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈɡɔs.tɐ d͡ʒi kaˈfɛ/ — /ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈɡos.tu/</span> — "Do you like coffee?" "No, I don't."

*Sim* is perfectly correct; it's just less common in casual speech. Its most natural spot is **after** the repeated verb, where it adds warmth or emphasis — much like English "Yes, I do!":

- *Gosto, sim.* <span class="ipa">/ˈɡos.tu ˈsĩ/</span> — I do, yes.
- *Sou, sim.* <span class="ipa">/ˈso(w) ˈsĩ/</span> — Yes, I am.

**3. "Right?" — *né*.** To check something you think is true, add *né* /ˈnɛ/ to the end of a statement. It's the spoken short form of *não é* /ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈɛ/ ("isn't it?") and never changes, whatever the verb or person:

- *Você é do Japão, né?* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈɛ du ʒaˈpɐ̃w̃ ˈnɛ/</span> — You're from Japan, right?

To confirm, you can repeat the verb (*Sou.*), or say *Isso.* <span class="ipa">/ˈi.su/</span> ("That's right."). For questions about how something is, *É.* <span class="ipa">/ˈɛ/</span> works as a general "yes."

## Watch out

> **Answer with your own verb form, not the questioner's.** The question uses *você* (he/she form); your answer uses *eu*.<br>
> ✗ *— Você fala inglês? — Fala.*<br>
> ✓ *— Você fala inglês? — Falo.* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈfa.lɐ ĩˈɡle(j)s/ — /ˈfa.lu/</span> — "Do you speak English?" "I do."

> **Keep the tense too.** A past-tense question gets a past-tense answer: *viu?* → *Vi.* <span class="ipa">/ˈvi/</span> ("I did.") You'll meet the past tense in Chapter 10; for now, just notice the pattern.

> **A bare *sim* can sound curt.** It isn't wrong, but on its own it may come across as stiff or formal in casual conversation. Repeating the verb, or adding *sim* after it, sounds friendlier.

## Quick check

<details markdown="1">
<summary>1. Turn into a question: <em>Ela é casada.</em> ("She's married.")</summary>

*Ela é casada?* <span class="ipa">/ˈɛ.lɐ ˈɛ kaˈza.dɐ/</span> — "Is she married?" Same words; only the intonation rises.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>2. Someone asks <em>Você gosta de café?</em> Answer "Yes, I do" the Brazilian way.</summary>

*Gosto.* <span class="ipa">/ˈɡos.tu/</span> or, warmer, *Gosto, sim.* <span class="ipa">/ˈɡos.tu ˈsĩ/</span> — "I do, yes."
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>3. Answer "No, I'm not" to <em>Você tá cansado?</em></summary>

*Não, não tô.* <span class="ipa">/ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈto/</span> — "No, I'm not."
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>4. What does <em>né?</em> add to <em>Você é brasileiro, né?</em></summary>

It turns the statement into a check, like "right?": "You're Brazilian, right?" It comes from *não é* /ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈɛ/ and never changes form.
</details>
