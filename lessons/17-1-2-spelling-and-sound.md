## A spelling you can trust

Portuguese spelling is far more regular than English. Once you know the rules below, you can read almost any word aloud — and the few gaps are listed at the end. (How final *-e*, *-o*, and *t/d* change was covered in 17-1-1.)

## Letters that depend on the next vowel

| Spelling | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *c* + a, o, u | /k/ | *copo*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈkɔ.pu/</span><br>glass, cup |
| *c* + e, i | /s/ | *cedo*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈse.du/</span><br>early |
| *ç* (only before a, o, u) | /s/ | *cabeça*<br><span class="ipa">/kaˈbe.sɐ/</span><br>head |
| *g* + a, o, u | /ɡ/ | *gato*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈɡa.tu/</span><br>cat |
| *g* + e, i | /ʒ/ | *gente*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈʒẽ.t͡ʃi/</span><br>people |
| *j* (any vowel) | /ʒ/ | *janela*<br><span class="ipa">/ʒaˈnɛ.lɐ/</span><br>window |
| *gu* + e, i (u silent) | /ɡ/ | *guerra*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈɡɛ.ʁɐ/</span><br>war |
| *qu* + e, i (u silent) | /k/ | *queijo*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈke(j).ʒu/</span><br>cheese |
| *qu* + a, o | /kw/ | *quatro*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈkwa.tɾu/</span><br>four |

## Two letters, one sound

| Spelling | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *ch* | /ʃ/ ("sh") | *chave*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈʃa.vi/</span><br>key |
| *lh* | /ʎ/ (like "lli" in *million*) | *filho*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈfi.ʎu/</span><br>son |
| *nh* | /ɲ/ (like "ni" in *onion*) | *vinho*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈvĩ.ɲu/</span><br>wine |
| *rr* | /ʁ/ (strong r) | *carro*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈka.ʁu/</span><br>car |
| *ss* | /s/ | *passo*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈpa.su/</span><br>step |

## Position matters

| Spelling | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *s* between vowels | /z/ | *casa*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈka.zɐ/</span><br>house |
| *r* at the start of a word | /ʁ/ (strong r) | *Rio*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈʁi.u/</span><br>Rio |
| *r* between vowels | /ɾ/ (tap) | *caro*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈka.ɾu/</span><br>expensive |
| *z* at the end of a word | /s/ | *feliz*<br><span class="ipa">/feˈlis/</span><br>happy |
| *m*, *n* after a vowel (not before a vowel) | nasal vowel | *tempo*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈtẽ.pu/</span><br>time, weather |
| *l* at the end of a syllable | /w/ | *sal*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈsaw/</span><br>salt |
| *h* | silent | *hoje*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈo.ʒi/</span><br>today |

## Accent marks

Accent marks show which syllable is stressed and, for *e* and *o*, whether the vowel is open or closed.

| Mark | What it shows | Example |
|---|---|---|
| acute: *á é í ó ú* | stress; *é*, *ó* are open | *café*<br><span class="ipa">/kaˈfɛ/</span><br>coffee |
| circumflex: *â ê ô* | stress; closed vowel | *você*<br><span class="ipa">/voˈse/</span><br>you |
| tilde: *ã õ* | nasal vowel | *mãe*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈmɐ̃j̃/</span><br>mother |

The grave accent *à* is different: it only shows that two *a*'s have merged (*a* "to" + *a* "the"), as in *A gente vai à praia.* <span class="ipa">/a ˈʒẽ.t͡ʃi ˈvaj a ˈpɾaj.ɐ/</span> — "We're going to the beach." It doesn't mark stress.

## What spelling doesn't tell you

- **Open or closed *e*/*o* without an accent.** *ele* /ˈe.li/ ("he") has a closed *e*; *ela* /ˈɛ.lɐ/ ("she") has an open one. You learn these word by word.
- **The letter *x*.** It has several sounds: /ʃ/ in *xícara* /ˈʃi.ka.ɾɐ/ ("cup") — the most common — /ks/ in *táxi* /ˈtak.si/, /z/ in *exame* /eˈzɐ̃.mi/ ("exam"), and /s/ in *próximo* /ˈpɾɔ.si.mu/ ("next").
- **Stress in words without an accent mark** follows a few default rules — see 17-4-1.

## Watch out

> **The *h* is always silent.** *hora* /ˈɔ.ɾɐ/ ("hour") and *hoje* /ˈo.ʒi/ ("today") begin with a vowel sound. The *h* only matters inside *ch*, *lh*, and *nh*.

> **Single *s* vs. *ss*.** Between vowels, one *s* is /z/ and *ss* is /s/: *casa* /ˈka.zɐ/ ("house") but *passo* /ˈpa.su/ ("step"). The double letter exists precisely to keep the /s/ sound.

> **The *m* at the end of *-em* and *-am* is not an English m.** In *bem* /ˈbẽj̃/ ("well"), the *m* isn't pronounced as a separate consonant: it tells you the vowel is nasal.

## Quick check

<details markdown="1">
<summary>1. How do you pronounce the <em>g</em> in <em>gente</em> and in <em>guerra</em>?</summary>

*gente* /ˈʒẽ.t͡ʃi/ has /ʒ/ (g before e); *guerra* /ˈɡɛ.ʁɐ/ has /ɡ/, because the silent *u* keeps the g hard.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>2. What sound does the <em>s</em> in <em>casa</em> make?</summary>

/z/ — a single *s* between vowels: *casa* /ˈka.zɐ/.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>3. Read <em>Que horas são?</em> aloud. Which letters are silent?</summary>

The *u* in *que* and the *h* in *horas*: *Que horas são?* <span class="ipa">/ki ˈɔ.ɾɐs ˈsɐ̃w̃/</span> — "What time is it?"
</details>
