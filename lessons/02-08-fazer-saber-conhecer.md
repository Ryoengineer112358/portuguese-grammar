## The forms

All three are irregular only in the *eu* form (and *sei* is the odd one out). *Faço* and *conheço* need the *ç* to keep the /s/ sound before *o*.

| Who | fazer ("to do, make") | saber ("to know") |
|---|---|---|
| eu | **faço**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈfa.su/</span> | **sei**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈsej/</span> |
| você · ele · ela · a gente | **faz**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈfa(j)s/</span> | **sabe**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈsa.bi/</span> |
| nós | **fazemos**<br><span class="ipa">/faˈzẽ.mus/</span> | **sabemos**<br><span class="ipa">/saˈbẽ.mus/</span> |
| vocês · eles · elas | **fazem**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈfa.zẽj̃/</span> | **sabem**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈsa.bẽj̃/</span> |

| Who | conhecer ("to know, be familiar with") |
|---|---|
| eu | **conheço**<br><span class="ipa">/kõˈɲe.su/</span> |
| você · ele · ela · a gente | **conhece**<br><span class="ipa">/kõˈɲɛ.si/</span> |
| nós | **conhecemos**<br><span class="ipa">/kõ.ɲeˈsẽ.mus/</span> |
| vocês · eles · elas | **conhecem**<br><span class="ipa">/kõˈɲɛ.sẽj̃/</span> |

## Three things to know

**1. *Fazer*: doing, and the weather.** *Fazer* covers both "do" and "make." It also describes the weather, with no subject — there's no word for "it":

- *O que você vai fazer hoje?* <span class="ipa">/u ˌki voˈse ˈvaj faˈze(ʁ) ˈo.ʒi/</span> — What are you going to do today?
- *Faz muito calor aqui.* <span class="ipa">/ˈfa(j)s ˈmũj̃.tu kaˈloʁ aˈki/</span> — It's really hot here.

*Faz* + a length of time means "for" or "ago": *Faz dois anos que moro aqui.* <span class="ipa">/ˈfa(j)s ˈdojs ˈɐ̃.nus ki ˈmɔ.ɾu aˈki/</span> — "I've lived here for two years."

**2. *Saber*: facts and skills.** Use *saber* for information — anything you could answer with a fact, often after *onde*, *quem*, *que* — and with an infinitive for something you know how to do:

- *Você sabe onde fica o banheiro?* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈsa.bi ˈõ.d͡ʒi ˈfi.kɐ u bɐ̃ˈɲe(j).ɾu/</span> — Do you know where the bathroom is?
- *Você sabe dirigir?* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈsa.bi d͡ʒi.ɾiˈʒi(ʁ)/</span> — Can you drive?

In casual speech, *Sei lá.* <span class="ipa">/ˈsej ˈla/</span> means "No idea" or "Beats me."

**3. *Conhecer*: people and places.** Use *conhecer* for someone you've met, and for a place you've been to:

- *Você conhece a Ana?* <span class="ipa">/voˈse kõˈɲɛ.si a ˈɐ̃.nɐ/</span> — Do you know Ana?
- *Você conhece o Rio?* <span class="ipa">/voˈse kõˈɲɛ.si u ˈʁi.u/</span> — Have you been to Rio?

## Watch out

> **People take *conhecer*, not *saber*.**<br>
> ✗ *Você sabe o meu irmão?*<br>
> ✓ *Você conhece o meu irmão?* <span class="ipa">/voˈse kõˈɲɛ.si u ˈmew iʁˈmɐ̃w̃/</span> — Do you know my brother?

> **No *como* after *saber*.** ✗ *Eu sei como nadar.* ✓ *Eu sei nadar.* <span class="ipa">/ˈew ˈsej naˈda(ʁ)/</span> — I can swim. (See also lesson 02-06: for a learned skill, *sei*, not *posso*.)

> ***Faz*, singular, for time.** Even with a plural, it's *faz*: *Faz dois anos.* <span class="ipa">/ˈfa(j)s ˈdojs ˈɐ̃.nus/</span> — "It's been two years." You may hear *fazem* in casual speech, but careful writers treat it as an error.

## Quick check

<details markdown="1">
<summary>1. <em>Saber</em> or <em>conhecer</em>? "Do you know São Paulo?" (= have you been there?)</summary>

*Conhecer*: *Você conhece São Paulo?* <span class="ipa">/voˈse kõˈɲɛ.si ˈsɐ̃w̃ ˈpaw.lu/</span> — "Have you been to São Paulo?"
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>2. <em>Saber</em> or <em>conhecer</em>? "Do you know what time it is?"</summary>

*Saber* — it's a fact: *Você sabe que horas são?* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈsa.bi ki ˈɔ.ɾɐs ˈsɐ̃w̃/</span> — "Do you know what time it is?"
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>3. Say "It's cold today." (<em>frio</em> = cold)</summary>

*Hoje faz frio.* <span class="ipa">/ˈo.ʒi ˈfa(j)s ˈfɾi.u/</span> — "It's cold today."
</details>
