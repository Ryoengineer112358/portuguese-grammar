## The pattern

*Gostar* /ɡosˈta(ʁ)/ ("to like") is a regular *-ar* verb (*gosto*, *gosta*, *gostamos*, *gostam*), but it always takes *de* /d͡ʒi/ ("of") before the thing you like. Think "be fond **of**."

| What follows | Example |
|---|---|
| a noun | *Gosto de café.*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈɡɔs.tu d͡ʒi kaˈfɛ/</span><br>I like coffee. |
| an infinitive | *Gosto de viajar.*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈɡɔs.tu d͡ʒi vi.aˈʒa(ʁ)/</span><br>I like travelling. |
| "the" + noun | *Gosto da praia.*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈɡɔs.tu da ˈpɾaj.ɐ/</span><br>I like the beach. |

*De* merges with the word after it. These are the ones you'll use most:

| de + | Contraction |
|---|---|
| o ("the", masc.) | **do**<br><span class="ipa">/du/</span> |
| a ("the", fem.) | **da**<br><span class="ipa">/da/</span> |
| os / as ("the", plural) | **dos** / **das**<br><span class="ipa">/dus/ /das/</span> |
| isso ("that") | **disso**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈd͡ʒi.su/</span> |
| aqui ("here") | **daqui**<br><span class="ipa">/daˈki/</span> |

## Three things to know

**1. Never drop *de* before what you like.** English "I like coffee" has no preposition, but Portuguese needs one every time: *Você gosta de música?* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈɡɔs.tɐ d͡ʒi ˈmu.zi.kɐ/</span> — "Do you like music?"

**2. …except when *gostar* ends the sentence.** In a short answer there's nothing after the verb, so there's no *de* either (lesson 01-06):

- *— Você gosta de chocolate? — Gosto!* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈɡɔs.tɐ d͡ʒi ʃo.koˈla.t͡ʃi/ — /ˈɡɔs.tu/</span> — "Do you like chocolate?" "I do!"

**3. Turning it up or down.** Put *muito* /ˈmũj̃.tu/ ("a lot") after the verb for "really like," and *não* before it for "don't like." For "love," use *adorar* /a.doˈɾa(ʁ)/ — which takes **no** *de*:

- *Gosto muito de viajar.* <span class="ipa">/ˈɡɔs.tu ˈmũj̃.tu d͡ʒi vi.aˈʒa(ʁ)/</span> — I really like travelling.
- *Não gosto de acordar cedo.* <span class="ipa">/ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈɡɔs.tu d͡ʒi a.koʁˈda(ʁ) ˈse.du/</span> — I don't like getting up early.
- *Adoro música brasileira.* <span class="ipa">/aˈdɔ.ɾu ˈmu.zi.kɐ bɾa.ziˈle(j).ɾɐ/</span> — I love Brazilian music.

*Odiar* /o.d͡ʒiˈa(ʁ)/ ("to hate") exists, but in conversation it sounds strong, so Brazilians use it sparingly.

## Watch out

> **Don't forget *de*.**<br>
> ✗ *Eu gosto café.*<br>
> ✓ *Eu gosto de café.* <span class="ipa">/ˈew ˈɡɔs.tu d͡ʒi kaˈfɛ/</span> — I like coffee.

> **But no *de* after *adorar*.** ✗ *Adoro de café.* ✓ *Adoro café.* <span class="ipa">/aˈdɔ.ɾu kaˈfɛ/</span> — I love coffee.

> **The person who likes is the subject** — not the thing liked, as in Spanish *me gusta*. *Eu gosto de gatos.* <span class="ipa">/ˈew ˈɡɔs.tu d͡ʒi ˈɡa.tus/</span> — "I like cats." The verb agrees with *eu*, never with *gatos*.

## Quick check

<details markdown="1">
<summary>1. Say "I like the beach." (<em>a praia</em> = the beach)</summary>

*Gosto da praia.* <span class="ipa">/ˈɡɔs.tu da ˈpɾaj.ɐ/</span> — "I like the beach." *De* + *a* = *da*.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>2. Someone asks <em>Você gosta de café?</em> Answer "Yes, a lot!"</summary>

*Gosto muito!* <span class="ipa">/ˈɡɔs.tu ˈmũj̃.tu/</span> — "I like it a lot!" No *de*, because nothing follows.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>3. Which is correct: <em>Adoro de viajar</em> or <em>Adoro viajar</em>?</summary>

*Adoro viajar.* <span class="ipa">/aˈdɔ.ɾu vi.aˈʒa(ʁ)/</span> — "I love travelling." *Adorar* takes no *de*.
</details>
