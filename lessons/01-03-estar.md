## The forms

*Estar* /isˈta(ʁ)/ is the other "to be." Where *ser* says what someone **is**, *estar* says how or where someone **is right now**.

In everyday speech, Brazilians drop the *es-* and say the short forms in the right-hand column. You will hear them constantly; the full forms are what you write.

| Who | Written | Spoken |
|---|---|---|
| eu | **estou**<br><span class="ipa">/isˈto(w)/</span> | **tô**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈto/</span> |
| você · ele · ela · a gente | **está**<br><span class="ipa">/isˈta/</span> | **tá**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈta/</span> |
| nós | **estamos**<br><span class="ipa">/isˈtɐ̃.mus/</span> | **tamos** / **tamo**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈtɐ̃.mus/</span> |
| vocês · eles · elas | **estão**<br><span class="ipa">/isˈtɐ̃w̃/</span> | **tão**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈtɐ̃w̃/</span> |

The first syllable is /is/ for most speakers and /iʃ/ in Rio de Janeiro (*estou* /iʃˈto(w)/); careful speech may have /es/.

## Three everyday jobs of *estar*

**1. How you are: greetings.** The standard Brazilian greeting is *Tudo bem?* <span class="ipa">/ˈtu.du ˈbẽj̃/</span> ("All good?"). The usual reply is simply *Tudo bem* or *Tudo bom* <span class="ipa">/ˈtu.du ˈbõ/</span> ("All good"), usually followed by *e você?* <span class="ipa">/i voˈse/</span> ("and you?") — whatever kind of day you're actually having. To ask more directly, say *Como você tá?* <span class="ipa">/ˈkõ.mu voˈse ˈta/</span> ("How are you?"); answer with *Tô bem.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto ˈbẽj̃/</span> ("I'm fine.")

**2. How you feel or what state you're in right now.** Temporary states — tired, busy, late, sick — take *estar*:

- *Tô cansado.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto kɐ̃ˈsa.du/</span> — I'm tired.
- *Tô atrasado.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto a.tɾaˈza.du/</span> — I'm late.

For hunger, cold, thirst, and similar sensations, Portuguese uses *estar com* + a noun, literally "to be with hunger": *Tô com fome.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto kõ ˈfõ.mi/</span> ("I'm hungry.")

**3. Where someone is right now.** *Onde você tá?* <span class="ipa">/ˈõ.d͡ʒi voˈse ˈta/</span> ("Where are you?") — *Tô em casa.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto ẽj̃ ˈka.zɐ/</span> ("I'm at home.") Use *estar* for where people and movable things are at the moment.

## Watch out

> **Say *tô*, write *estou*.** The short forms are normal speech, not slang, and you'll see them in texts and chat. In emails, forms, and anything formal, write the full forms.

> **"I'm hungry" and "I'm cold" use *estar com*.** English uses an adjective, but Portuguese uses a noun: *Tô com fome.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto kõ ˈfõ.mi/</span> ("I'm hungry," lit. "I'm with hunger") · *Tô com frio.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto kõ ˈfɾi.u/</span> ("I'm cold.")

> ***Tá* on its own means "OK."** *Tá bom.* <span class="ipa">/ˈta ˈbõ/</span> or just *Tá.* <span class="ipa">/ˈta/</span> is the everyday "OK / sounds good." At the end of a sentence, *tá?* means "OK?": *Depois eu falo com você, tá?* <span class="ipa">/deˈpojs ˈew ˈfa.lu kõ voˈse ˈta/</span> ("I'll talk to you later, OK?")
