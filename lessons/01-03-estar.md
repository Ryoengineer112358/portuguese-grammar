## The forms

*Estar* /isˈta(ʁ)/ is the other "to be." Where *ser* says what someone **is**, *estar* says how or where someone **is right now**.

In everyday speech, Brazilians drop the *es-* and say the short forms on the right. You will hear them constantly; the full forms are what you write.

| Who | Written | IPA | Spoken | IPA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| eu | **estou** | /isˈto(w)/ | **tô** | /ˈto/ |
| você · ele · ela · a gente | **está** | /isˈta/ | **tá** | /ˈta/ |
| nós | **estamos** | /isˈtɐ̃.mus/ | **tamos** / **tamo** | /ˈtɐ̃.mus/ |
| vocês · eles · elas | **estão** | /isˈtɐ̃w̃/ | **tão** | /ˈtɐ̃w̃/ |

The first syllable is /is/ for most speakers and /iʃ/ in Rio de Janeiro (*estou* /iʃˈto(w)/); careful speech may have /es/.

## Three everyday jobs of *estar*

**1. How you are: greetings.** The standard Brazilian greeting is *Tudo bem?* ("All good?"). The usual reply is simply *Tudo bem* or *Tudo bom*, usually followed by *e você?* — whatever kind of day you're actually having. To ask more directly, say *Como você tá?* ("How are you?"); answer with *Tô bem* ("I'm fine").

**2. How you feel or what state you're in right now.** Temporary states — tired, busy, late, sick — take *estar*: *Tô cansado* ("I'm tired"), *Tô atrasado* ("I'm late"). For hunger, cold, thirst, and similar sensations, Portuguese uses *estar com* + a noun, literally "to be with hunger": *Tô com fome* ("I'm hungry").

**3. Where someone is right now.** *Onde você tá?* ("Where are you?") — *Tô em casa* ("I'm at home"). Use *estar* for where people and movable things are at the moment.

## Watch out

> **Say *tô*, write *estou*.** The short forms are normal speech, not slang, and you'll see them in texts and chat. In emails, forms, and anything formal, write the full forms.

> **"I'm hungry" and "I'm cold" use *estar com*.** English uses an adjective, but Portuguese uses a noun: *Tô com fome* (lit. "I'm with hunger"), *Tô com frio* /ˈto kõ ˈfɾi.u/ ("I'm cold").

> ***Tá* on its own means "OK."** *Tá bom* or just *Tá* is the everyday "OK / sounds good." At the end of a sentence, *tá?* means "OK?": *Te ligo depois, tá?* ("I'll call you later, OK?").
