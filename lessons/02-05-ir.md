## The forms

*Ir* /ˈi(ʁ)/ ("to go") is completely irregular — none of its present forms look like the infinitive:

| Who | ir |
|---|---|
| eu | **vou**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈvo(w)/</span> |
| você · ele · ela · a gente | **vai**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈvaj/</span> |
| nós | **vamos**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈvɐ̃.mus/</span> |
| vocês · eles · elas | **vão**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈvɐ̃w̃/</span> |

## Three things to know

**1. Going somewhere: *pra*, *pro*, *no*, *na*.** In speech, the "to" after *ir* is usually *pra* /pɾa/, the short form of *para* /ˈpa.ɾɐ/. Before a masculine noun with "the," *para o* becomes *pro* /pɾu/; before a feminine one, *para a* is still just *pra*:

- *Vou pra casa.* <span class="ipa">/ˈvo(w) pɾa ˈka.zɐ/</span> — I'm going home.
- *Vou pro trabalho.* <span class="ipa">/ˈvo(w) pɾu tɾaˈba.ʎu/</span> — I'm going to work.
- *Você vai pra praia hoje?* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈvaj pɾa ˈpɾaj.ɐ ˈo.ʒi/</span> — Are you going to the beach today?

You'll also hear *no* /nu/ and *na* /na/ ("in the") used the same way: *Vou no mercado.* <span class="ipa">/ˈvo(w) nu meʁˈka.du/</span> — "I'm going to the supermarket." Formal writing uses *a* (*ao*, *à*) instead.

**2. *Vou* + infinitive = the future.** This is how Brazilians normally talk about the future — both "I'm going to" and "I will." Put the infinitive straight after *ir*, with nothing in between:

- *Vou comer agora.* <span class="ipa">/ˈvo(w) koˈme(ʁ) aˈɡɔ.ɾɐ/</span> — I'm going to eat now.
- *Vai chover amanhã.* <span class="ipa">/ˈvaj ʃoˈve(ʁ) a.mɐ̃ˈɲɐ̃/</span> — It's going to rain tomorrow.

Portuguese also has a one-word future tense, but in conversation it sounds formal. Lesson 05-01 looks at *vou* + infinitive in more detail.

**3. *Vamos* = "let's."** *Vamos* + infinitive is a suggestion, and *Vamos!* <span class="ipa">/ˈvɐ̃.mus/</span> alone means "Let's go!" Among friends you'll often hear *Bora!* <span class="ipa">/ˈbɔ.ɾɐ/</span> ("Let's go!") instead.

- *Vamos tomar um café?* <span class="ipa">/ˈvɐ̃.mus toˈma(ʁ) ũ kaˈfɛ/</span> — Shall we grab a coffee?

## Watch out

> **No *a* before the infinitive.**<br>
> ✗ *Vou a comer.*<br>
> ✓ *Vou comer.* <span class="ipa">/ˈvo(w) koˈme(ʁ)/</span> — I'm going to eat.

> **Don't say *vou ir*.** To say "I'm going to go," just use *vou* with the place: *Amanhã vou na festa.* <span class="ipa">/a.mɐ̃ˈɲɐ̃ ˈvo(w) na ˈfɛs.tɐ/</span> — "I'm going to (go to) the party tomorrow."

> ***A gente vai*, not *a gente vamos*.** As with every verb, *a gente* takes the *você* form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>2. What's the difference between <em>Vamos!</em> and <em>Vamos comer?</em></summary>

*Vamos!* <span class="ipa">/ˈvɐ̃.mus/</span> — "Let's go!" *Vamos comer?* <span class="ipa">/ˈvɐ̃.mus koˈme(ʁ)/</span> — "Shall we eat?" With an infinitive, *vamos* suggests doing that thing.
