## The basic rule: *não* goes right before the verb

*Não* /ˈnɐ̃w̃/ means both "no" and "not." To make a sentence negative, put *não* directly in front of the verb. Nothing else changes — there's no helper verb like English *don't*.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| *Eu falo inglês.*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈew ˈfa.lu ĩˈɡle(j)s/</span><br>I speak English. | *Eu **não** falo inglês.*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈew ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈfa.lu ĩˈɡle(j)s/</span><br>I don't speak English. |
| *Sou brasileiro.*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈso(w) bɾa.ziˈle(j).ɾu/</span><br>I'm Brazilian. | ***Não** sou brasileiro.*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈso(w) bɾa.ziˈle(j).ɾu/</span><br>I'm not Brazilian. |
| *Ela tá em casa.*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈɛ.lɐ ˈta ẽj̃ ˈka.zɐ/</span><br>She's at home. | *Ela **não** tá em casa.*<br><span class="ipa">/ˈɛ.lɐ ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈta ẽj̃ ˈka.zɐ/</span><br>She's not at home. |

## Three things to know

**1. Saying "no" to a question.** Start with *Não,* and then give the negative sentence — so you often hear *não* twice in a row:

- *— Você é brasileiro? — Não, não sou.* <span class="ipa">/voˈse ˈɛ bɾa.ziˈle(j).ɾu/ — /ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈso(w)/</span> — "Are you Brazilian?" "No, I'm not."

The first *não* is "no"; the second is "not." (Short answers in general are the topic of the next lesson.)

**2. The Brazilian extra *não* at the end.** In relaxed speech, Brazilians often add another *não* at the end of a short sentence. It softens or emphasizes the "no" and is not a mistake — but it's informal, and it only works in short, simple sentences. You'll also hear the first *não* dropped altogether:

- *Não sei.* <span class="ipa">/ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈsej/</span> — I don't know. (neutral, always correct)
- *Não sei, não.* <span class="ipa">/ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈsej ˈnɐ̃w̃/</span> — I really don't know. (casual)
- *Sei não.* <span class="ipa">/ˈsej ˈnɐ̃w̃/</span> — Dunno. (very casual)

Understand all three; use the first one until it feels natural to do otherwise.

**3. *Nada*, *ninguém*, *nunca*.** "Nothing" *nada* /ˈna.dɐ/, "nobody" *ninguém* /nĩˈɡẽj̃/, and "never" *nunca* /ˈnũ.kɐ/ work differently from English:

- **After the verb**, they need *não* before the verb as well — a "double negative" that is perfectly correct in Portuguese:
  *Não tem nada aqui.* <span class="ipa">/ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈtẽj̃ ˈna.dɐ aˈki/</span> — There's nothing here.
- **Before the verb**, they replace *não*:
  *Ninguém sabe.* <span class="ipa">/nĩˈɡẽj̃ ˈsa.bi/</span> — Nobody knows. · *Nunca fui ao Rio.* <span class="ipa">/ˈnũ.kɐ ˈfuj aw ˈʁi.u/</span> — I've never been to Rio.

## Watch out

> ***Não, não…* is not a stammer.** The first *não* answers the question; the second negates the verb. Brazilians may even add a third at the end: *Não, não vai chover, não.* <span class="ipa">/ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈvaj ʃoˈve(ʁ) ˈnɐ̃w̃/</span> — "No, it's not going to rain."

> **No *não* when *ninguém* or *nada* comes first.**<br>
> ✗ *Ninguém não sabe.*<br>
> ✓ *Ninguém sabe.* <span class="ipa">/nĩˈɡẽj̃ ˈsa.bi/</span> — Nobody knows.

> **Keep the final *não* for short sentences.** *Não sei, não* is natural; tacking *não* onto the end of a long sentence sounds odd. In writing and formal speech, use only the *não* before the verb.

## Quick check

<details markdown="1">
<summary>1. Make this negative: <em>Eu falo espanhol.</em> ("I speak Spanish.")</summary>

*Eu não falo espanhol.* <span class="ipa">/ˈew ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈfa.lu is.pɐ̃ˈɲɔw/</span> — I don't speak Spanish.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>2. How do you say "I don't know anyone here"?</summary>

*Não conheço ninguém aqui.* <span class="ipa">/ˈnɐ̃w̃ kõˈɲe.su nĩˈɡẽj̃ aˈki/</span> — the *não* before the verb is required.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>3. A friend answers <em>Sei não.</em> What do they mean, and how formal is it?</summary>

"I don't know" / "No idea." It's very casual; the neutral form is *Não sei.* <span class="ipa">/ˈnɐ̃w̃ ˈsej/</span>
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>4. Which is correct: <em>Ninguém sabe</em> or <em>Ninguém não sabe</em>?</summary>

*Ninguém sabe.* <span class="ipa">/nĩˈɡẽj̃ ˈsa.bi/</span> — "Nobody knows." When *ninguém* comes before the verb, there's no *não*.
</details>
