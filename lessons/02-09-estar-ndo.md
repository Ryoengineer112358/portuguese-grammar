## The form

The gerund (the *-ndo* form, like English *-ing*) is the easiest form in Portuguese: take the infinitive, drop the final *-r*, and add *-ndo*. It works for regular and irregular verbs alike:

| Infinitive | Gerund |
|---|---|
| **falar**<br><span class="ipa">/faˈla(ʁ)/</span> | **falando**<br><span class="ipa">/faˈlɐ̃.du/</span> |
| **comer**<br><span class="ipa">/koˈme(ʁ)/</span> | **comendo**<br><span class="ipa">/koˈmẽ.du/</span> |
| **abrir**<br><span class="ipa">/aˈbɾi(ʁ)/</span> | **abrindo**<br><span class="ipa">/aˈbɾĩ.du/</span> |
| **fazer**<br><span class="ipa">/faˈze(ʁ)/</span> | **fazendo**<br><span class="ipa">/faˈzẽ.du/</span> |
| **ir**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈi(ʁ)/</span> | **indo**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈĩ.du/</span> |

Put it after *estar* — in speech, the short forms *tô*, *tá*, *tamo*, *tão* from lesson 01-03:

- *Tô trabalhando.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto tɾa.baˈʎɐ̃.du/</span> — I'm working.
- *Tá chovendo.* <span class="ipa">/ˈta ʃoˈvẽ.du/</span> — It's raining.

There are very few exceptions; the one you'll meet is *pôr* /ˈpo(ʁ)/ ("to put"), whose gerund is *pondo* /ˈpõ.du/.

## Three things to know

**1. Right now.** *Estar* + *-ndo* is how Brazilians describe what's happening at this moment:

- *O que você tá fazendo?* <span class="ipa">/u ˌki voˈse ˈta faˈzẽ.du/</span> — What are you doing?

**2. These days.** It also covers a temporary phase of life, like English "I'm learning Portuguese":

- *Tô aprendendo português.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto a.pɾẽˈdẽ.du poʁ.tuˈɡe(j)s/</span> — I'm learning Portuguese.

**3. Habits and settled facts keep the plain present.** Compare:

- *Eu moro em São Paulo.* <span class="ipa">/ˈew ˈmɔ.ɾu ẽj̃ ˈsɐ̃w̃ ˈpaw.lu/</span> — I live in São Paulo. (it's my home)
- *Tô morando em São Paulo.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto moˈɾɐ̃.du ẽj̃ ˈsɐ̃w̃ ˈpaw.lu/</span> — I'm living in São Paulo. (for now)

Likewise, *Eu como carne.* <span class="ipa">/ˈew ˈkõ.mu ˈkaʁ.ni/</span> — "I eat meat" (in general), but *Tô comendo.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto koˈmẽ.du/</span> — "I'm eating" (right now).

## Watch out

> **Brazil uses *-ndo*, not *a* + infinitive.** In Portugal you'll hear *estou a comer*; in Brazil that sounds European. Brazilians say *estou comendo* in every register, formal or casual.<br>
> ✓ *Tô comendo.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto koˈmẽ.du/</span> — I'm eating.

> **Don't forget *estar*, and make it agree.** ✗ *Está chove.* ✗ *Eles está dormindo.*<br>
> ✓ *Eles tão dormindo.* <span class="ipa">/ˈe.lis ˈtɐ̃w̃ doʁˈmĩ.du/</span> — They're sleeping.

> **Don't use it for a permanent home or a habit.** *Eu trabalho num banco.* <span class="ipa">/ˈew tɾaˈba.ʎu ˈnũ ˈbɐ̃.ku/</span> — "I work at a bank" (my job). *Tô trabalhando.* <span class="ipa">/ˈto tɾa.baˈʎɐ̃.du/</span> — "I'm working" (now).
