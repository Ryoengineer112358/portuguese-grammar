## The forms

Many of the most common Portuguese verbs end in *-ar* in the infinitive (the dictionary form, "to …"). To use one of these in the present tense, take off *-ar* and add one of four endings. *Falar* /faˈla(ʁ)/ ("to speak") becomes:

| Who | Ending | falar |
|---|---|---|
| eu | -o | **falo**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈfa.lu/</span> |
| você · ele · ela · a gente | -a | **fala**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈfa.lɐ/</span> |
| nós | -amos | **falamos**<br><span class="ipa">/faˈlɐ̃.mus/</span> |
| vocês · eles · elas | -am | **falam**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈfa.lɐ̃w̃/</span> |

Every regular *-ar* verb works the same way. Here are *morar* /moˈɾa(ʁ)/ ("to live") and *trabalhar* /tɾa.baˈʎa(ʁ)/ ("to work"):

| Who | morar | trabalhar |
|---|---|---|
| eu | **moro**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈmɔ.ɾu/</span> | **trabalho**<br><span class="ipa">/tɾaˈba.ʎu/</span> |
| você · ele · ela · a gente | **mora**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈmɔ.ɾɐ/</span> | **trabalha**<br><span class="ipa">/tɾaˈba.ʎɐ/</span> |
| nós | **moramos**<br><span class="ipa">/moˈɾɐ̃.mus/</span> | **trabalhamos**<br><span class="ipa">/tɾa.baˈʎɐ̃.mus/</span> |
| vocês · eles · elas | **moram**<br><span class="ipa">/ˈmɔ.ɾɐ̃w̃/</span> | **trabalham**<br><span class="ipa">/tɾaˈba.ʎɐ̃w̃/</span> |

In practice you only need four forms. *Tu* and *vós* have their own endings in grammar books, but Brazilians normally don't use them.

## Three things to know

**1. One pattern for the whole family.** The stem (*fal-*, *mor-*, *trabalh-*) never changes; only the ending does. Learn the pattern once and it works for *estudar* /is.tuˈda(ʁ)/ ("to study"), *ligar* /liˈɡa(ʁ)/ ("to call"), *chegar* /ʃeˈɡa(ʁ)/ ("to arrive"), and nearly every other *-ar* verb. A few, such as *estar* (lesson 01-03), are irregular.

**2. *A gente* takes the *você* form.** *A gente* means "we," but its verb ends in *-a*, like *você* and *ela* — never in *-amos*:

- *A gente mora aqui.* <span class="ipa">/a ˈʒẽ.t͡ʃi ˈmɔ.ɾɐ aˈki/</span> — We live here.

**3. The present covers more than English "I do."** It's used for habits and facts, and — with a time word such as *amanhã* /a.mɐ̃ˈɲɐ̃/ ("tomorrow") — for plans in the near future:

- *Trabalho todo dia.* <span class="ipa">/tɾaˈba.ʎu ˈto.du ˈd͡ʒi.ɐ/</span> — I work every day.
- *Te ligo amanhã.* <span class="ipa">/t͡ʃi ˈli.ɡu a.mɐ̃ˈɲɐ̃/</span> — I'll call you tomorrow.

For something in progress at this very moment, Brazilians usually say *estar* + *-ndo* instead (*Tô trabalhando*, "I'm working"), the topic of lesson 02-09.

## Watch out

> **No *-amos* with *a gente*.**<br>
> ✗ *A gente falamos português.*<br>
> ✓ *A gente fala português.* <span class="ipa">/a ˈʒẽ.t͡ʃi ˈfa.lɐ poʁ.tuˈɡe(j)s/</span> — We speak Portuguese.

> ***Falamos* is also "we spoke."** In Brazil the *nós* form of the present is identical to the past (preterite). Context decides: *Falamos ontem.* <span class="ipa">/faˈlɐ̃.mus ˈõ.tẽj̃/</span> — "We talked yesterday."

> **A stressed *o* in the stem often opens.** In *morar* /moˈɾa(ʁ)/ the stress is on the ending and the *o* is closed, but in *moro* /ˈmɔ.ɾu/ and *mora* /ˈmɔ.ɾɐ/ the stress moves to the stem and the *o* opens to /ɔ/. That's why the verb *gosto* /ˈɡɔs.tu/ ("I like") sounds different from the noun *gosto* /ˈɡos.tu/ ("taste").
