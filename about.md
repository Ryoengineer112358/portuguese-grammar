# About this book

This is a grammar of **Brazilian Portuguese** for learners whose first goal is everyday conversation. It grows one lesson at a time; the [contents page]({{ '/' | relative_url }}) shows the full plan, with finished lessons marked ✓.

## How lessons are organized

- Each lesson covers one point and takes about five minutes.
- Chapters are ordered by how soon you need them in conversation, not by traditional grammar categories.
- Example sentences are short and are things people actually say.
- When colloquial Brazilian usage differs from formal written norms, lessons teach the spoken form first and point out the formal one.

## Sources and verification

Every lesson ends with the sources it was checked against and a status label:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 Verified | Agrees across at least two independent sources **and** has been reviewed by a native speaker or teacher. |
| 🟡 Source-checked | Agrees across at least two independent sources; not yet reviewed by a person. |
| 🟠 Needs checking | Based on a single source, or contains a point the sources disagree on. |

The overall chapter plan was cross-checked against the structure of John Whitlam, *Modern Brazilian Portuguese Grammar: A Practical Guide* (Routledge), which is organized into grammatical structures (Part A) and communicative functions (Part B).

If you spot an error, please open an issue on the GitHub repository.
